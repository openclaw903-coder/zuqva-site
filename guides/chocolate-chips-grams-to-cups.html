How many grams in a cup of chocolate chips?

One level US cup of standard semisweet chocolate chips weighs 170 grams, from the King Arthur Baking ingredient weight chart. USDA FoodData Central lists the same cup at 168 grams, so the two sources sit 2 grams apart. That cup carries about 816 calories. The number only holds for standard size chips, spooned into the cup and levelled. Mini chips pack tighter and weigh more, and big chunks leave air gaps and weigh less.

Standard chips by the cup, spoon and fraction

Every figure is for one level US cup, 236.6 millilitres, of standard semisweet chocolate chips, using 170 grams a cup from King Arthur Baking. USDA lists semisweet chocolate at 480 calories per 100 grams, with 30 grams of fat, 64 grams of carbohydrate and 4.2 grams of protein, so the calories below follow from that per 100 gram figure.

One cup holds 16 tablespoons, and 100 grams is just over half a cup. A standard 12-ounce bag holds 340 grams, about 2 cups, so a recipe that calls for 1 cup uses half a bag. A 6-ounce bag is 170 grams, exactly 1 cup. Milk chocolate bags often run 11.5 ounces, 326 grams, just under 2 cups, so check the bag before assuming it holds two full cups.

Why the same cup can weigh 168 grams or 182 grams

The gap is size, not a mistake. USDA FoodData Central, record 167976 for semisweet chocolate, measures the cup three ways. A cup of chips from a 6-ounce package is 168 grams. A cup of mini chips is 173 grams. A cup of large chips is 182 grams. King Arthur Baking gives 170 grams for standard chips and 177 grams for mini chips. The two sources agree within 4 grams on each size, and both put mini chips above standard chips by the same amount.

Small pieces pack with fewer gaps, so a cup holds more chocolate. Large pieces leave more air between them, so a cup holds less, except that USDA large chips are still solid drops and weigh 182 grams. Irregular chunks are a different case. King Arthur lists chopped chocolate at 170 grams a cup, the same as standard chips, while converter sites that weigh rough jumbo chunks list 145 to 156 grams a cup. A chopped bar settles fairly evenly, a bag of rough chunks does not, so weigh chunks instead of trusting the cup.

Brands add a smaller spread. One converter site lists Nestle Toll House at 170 grams a cup, Hershey at 168, Ghirardelli at 175 and Guittard extra dark at 172, a 7 gram spread. The type of chocolate matters less than the size: milk, dark and white chips in the standard drop shape all sit at about 170 grams a cup on the King Arthur chart, because the shape decides how much air is in the cup, not the cocoa percentage.

Mini chips are the ones that catch people out. A cup of them weighs about 4 percent more than a cup of standard chips, so swapping them into a recipe written for standard chips adds about 7 grams of chocolate a cup. That is small in cookies and worth knowing in a cake, where the chips are part of the structure. Rough chunks run the other way: a cup can hold 15 to 25 grams less chocolate than a cup of standard chips.

Worked example: a batch of chocolate chip cookies

A classic chocolate chip cookie recipe calls for 2 cups of standard chips. That is 340 grams, the whole 12-ounce bag, and about 1,632 calories of chocolate in the batch. Cut into 24 cookies, the chips alone add about 68 calories to each cookie.

Use mini chips and 2 cups weigh about 354 grams, so the batch gains about 14 grams of chocolate and about 67 calories. Use rough chunks and 2 cups weigh about 290 to 312 grams, so the batch loses 28 to 50 grams of chocolate. Weighing the chips takes the size of the piece out of the recipe entirely.

Logging chips without logging the wrong amount

A cup of standard semisweet chips carries about 816 calories, 51 grams of fat, 109 grams of carbohydrate and 7.1 grams of protein. Two tablespoons over oatmeal or yoghurt weigh about 21 grams and add about 102 calories, easy to forget when it happens every morning.

The entry has to match the form you weighed, the same as with dry and cooked grains. Log a cup of mini chips as standard chips and you count about 816 calories instead of 850. Log a cup of rough chunks as standard chips and you count about 816 instead of about 700, an overcount of more than 100 calories. Work out what the portion means for your day in the TDEE calculator.

US cup, metric cup and UK cup

A US cup holds 236.6 millilitres, so 170 grams of standard chips. A metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand and Canada, holds 250 millilitres, about 6 percent more, so about 180 grams of standard chips, 187 grams of mini chips and 192 grams of large chips. A UK cup is also 250 millilitres in modern recipes, so use the metric figures there too. When a recipe gives grams, use the grams and skip the cup entirely.

In a Pakistani kitchen

Chocolate chips turn up most often in cookies, brownies and muffins, and in banana bread. Supermarkets in Pakistan sell them as morsels or chips, usually semisweet, and local brands vary a little in piece size. Weigh the chips when a recipe has to come out the same twice, and use 170 grams as the starting point for standard size chips.
