How many grams of coffee per cup?

Use 13 to 15 grams of ground coffee for one 8 oz mug. That is the answer for a normal measuring cup of water, and it comes from the Specialty Coffee Association, which recommends 55 to 60 grams of coffee per litre of water, about 1 part coffee to 17 or 18 parts water. At 55 grams a litre, an 8 oz mug takes 13 grams. At 60 grams a litre, it takes 14 grams. Most home brewers land at 15 grams, which is a 1 to 16 ratio, a little stronger than the SCA figure and the amount most charts quote for a mug.

Coffee by the cup, mug and pot

Every figure is grams of ground coffee for the water listed, at the SCA range of 55 to 60 grams per litre. Water weighs about 1 gram per millilitre, so the millilitres and the grams of water are the same number.

A level tablespoon of ground coffee weighs about 5 grams, so 15 grams is about 3 level tablespoons and a standard coffee scoop, which holds 2 tablespoons, is about 10 grams. The spoon is only an estimate. A tablespoon can weigh 4 to 7 grams depending on the roast and the grind, so two people following the same spoon recipe can end up 3 or 4 grams apart on one mug. Weigh the coffee once, note where it sits in your own scoop, and use that scoop after.

Why one cup means three things

The confusion is the word cup. A US measuring cup holds 8 fluid ounces, 237 millilitres. A coffee maker counts a cup as 5 or 6 fluid ounces, 148 or 177 millilitres, depending on the brand. A 12 cup machine therefore holds 60 to 72 ounces of water, not 96, and a 10 cup pot at 5 ounce lines holds about 1.48 litres, which needs 81 to 89 grams of coffee at the SCA ratio. At 6 ounce lines the same pot holds about 1.77 litres and needs 98 to 106 grams. Count the water, not the number printed on the carafe.

A French press counts its cups smaller still, about 4 ounces each, which is why a 3 cup press makes one generous mug. The fix is the same in every case. Fill the brewer to the level you actually use, pour that water into a measuring jug, and work out the coffee from the millilitres. A mug is no better. Most mugs hold 12 ounces, not 8, so a recipe written for an 8 ounce cup underdoses a full mug by about a third.

Stronger or lighter, by a gram or two

Start at 13 grams for an 8 ounce mug and move in steps of 1 or 2 grams. Lighter means 12 or 13 grams, stronger means 15 to 17 grams. Past 17 grams an 8 ounce cup turns heavy and bitter for most people, and below 12 it tastes thin. Change one thing at a time. If the cup tastes sour and weak, add coffee. If it tastes bitter and dry, use a little less, or grind a little coarser, instead of adding more coffee to fix it.

The method shifts the starting point a little. Pour over usually runs stronger, 15 to 18 grams per 240 millilitres, inside the National Coffee Association range of 1 to 13 to 1 to 16. French press sits near 16 grams for the same water, about 1 to 15. Drip machines follow the SCA figure, 13 to 14 grams. Espresso is a different drink entirely. One shot uses about 18 grams of coffee for about 30 grams of drink out, and it is not a cup of brewed coffee scaled down.

Worked example: a morning pot and a bag of beans

A 10 cup machine filled to the 10 line at 5 ounces a cup holds about 1.48 litres of water, so it needs about 81 to 89 grams of ground coffee. Fill the same machine to the 10 line at 6 ounces a cup and it holds about 1.77 litres, needing about 98 to 106 grams. The machine number never changed. Only the size of its cup did.

A 250 gram bag of beans makes about 17 to 19 mugs at 13 to 15 grams a mug. A 12 ounce bag, 340 grams, makes about 23 to 26 mugs, and a 1 pound bag, 454 grams, makes about 30 to 35. Weigh the beans before grinding, not after, because whole beans and ground coffee pack differently in a spoon.

What a cup of coffee actually weighs

One 8 oz cup of black brewed coffee weighs 237 grams, from USDA FoodData Central record 171890, and carries about 2 calories and about 95 milligrams of caffeine. A 6 oz cup weighs 178 grams with about 71 milligrams of caffeine. A single shot of espresso weighs about 30 grams, carries about 3 calories and about 63 milligrams of caffeine. The drink itself is almost calorie free. Milk, sugar and syrup are where the calories come in, so log those separately.

The grounds keep some of the water. Ground coffee absorbs about twice its own weight, so 15 grams of grounds hold back about 30 grams of water and the mug ends up holding about 207 grams of drink from 237 grams poured in. That is why recipes give the water going in, not the coffee coming out. Weigh the water you pour, and let the drink be what it is.

US cup, metric cup and UK cup

A US cup holds 236.6 millilitres, so 13 to 14 grams of coffee at the SCA ratio. A metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand and Canada, holds 250 millilitres, so 14 to 15 grams. A UK mug is usually larger than either, 300 to 350 millilitres, so 17 to 21 grams. When a recipe gives grams, use the grams and skip the cup entirely.

In a Pakistani kitchen

Coffee is still a small habit next to chai in most Pakistani homes, and most of it is instant coffee stirred into hot milk or water. Ground coffee is sold in supermarkets and coffee shops in the bigger cities, and the same gram figures apply to it. For a milk coffee, brew the coffee first at the figures above, then add milk, and weigh the milk separately if you are logging it.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams of coffee per cup?

13 to 15 grams of ground coffee for an 8 oz mug of water, from the Specialty Coffee Association range of 55 to 60 grams per litre. Use 15 to 16 grams for a stronger cup.

How many grams of coffee for a 6 oz coffee maker cup?

About 10 grams. A coffee maker cup is 6 fluid ounces, 177 millilitres, not 8 ounces.

How many tablespoons of coffee is 15 grams?

About 3 level tablespoons. A level tablespoon of ground coffee weighs about 5 grams, though it can run 4 to 7 grams with roast and grind.

How much does a cup of brewed coffee weigh?

237 grams for one 8 oz cup of black brewed coffee, from USDA FoodData Central. It carries about 2 calories and about 95 milligrams of caffeine.

Every figure on this page is approximate, because roast, grind and how a spoon is filled all move the weight a little. For exact work, weigh the coffee and the water.
