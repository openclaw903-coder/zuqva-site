How many grams in a cup of heavy cream?

Last updated: 28 Nov 2026.

One level US cup of liquid heavy cream weighs 238 grams and carries about 809 calories, from USDA FoodData Central. Whipped, one cup of the same cream weighs 120 grams and carries about 408 calories. A cup of liquid cream and a cup of whipped cream are not the same amount of cream.

Why King Arthur says 227 grams and USDA says 238

King Arthur Baking lists cream at 227 grams a cup, and a lot of charts copy that number. The reason is in the fine print: King Arthur puts heavy cream, light cream and half-and-half on one shared line at 227 grams, which is simply 8 ounces by weight. It is a baking shorthand, not a measurement of heavy cream on its own.

USDA measures heavy whipping cream as its own food, FDC 170859, and gets 238 grams for one fluid cup. The gap is 11 grams, about 5 percent. In a soup it will not show; in a ganache or a whipped topping, use 238 grams. If a recipe gives its own gram amounts, follow those instead of any chart.

Liquid versus whipped: the same cream, half the weight per cup

Whipping beats air into cream. The weight stays the same and the volume doubles, so one cup of liquid cream, 238 grams, whips to about 2 cups. Soft peaks come out a little under 2 cups and stiff peaks a little over. A cup of whipped cream weighs 120 grams because about half of what fills the cup is air.

Calories per gram never change: 340 per 100 grams, poured or whipped. What changes is how much cream fits in the cup, so a whipped cup holds about half the calories of a liquid one, 408 against 809.

The cream ladder

Cream is sold by fat, and the fat decides what it can do. Only cream with 30 percent fat or more whips to stiff peaks. In the US, heavy cream must contain at least 36 percent milkfat by FDA rule, and "heavy cream" and "heavy whipping cream" are the same product on the carton.

The ladder weights come from converter references rather than USDA, and they sit within a few grams of each other. A cup of any pouring cream weighs about the same; the fat is what differs. Single cream or half-and-half will not whip, however long you beat it, because there is not enough fat to hold the air.

Worked example: whipped cream for a cake

Frosting a single-layer 8 or 9 inch cake takes about 1 and a half cups of whipped cream. Start with 3/4 cup of liquid cream, 179 grams, straight from the fridge, whipped cold with a spoonful of powdered sugar and a splash of vanilla.

For a ganache, weigh equal parts: 1 cup of cream, 238 grams, poured hot over 238 grams of chopped dark chocolate, stirred until smooth. That ratio only holds by weight, because a cup of cream and a cup of chocolate do not weigh the same.

Logging liquid as whipped, or the reverse

A cup of heavy cream carries 809 calories, 85.9 grams of fat and 6.8 grams of protein, from the same USDA entry. Two tablespoons in morning coffee weigh 30 grams and add about 102 calories, about 714 calories over a week. A spoonful of whipped cream on dessert looks light and still costs about 408 calories a cup.

The entry has to match the state you weighed, the same mistake as with dry and cooked grains. Weigh liquid cream and log it as whipped, and you count about half the real calories; weigh whipped and log it liquid, and you count about double. A quick substitute when the carton is empty

For sauces, soups and baking, whisk 3/4 cup of whole milk with 1/4 cup of melted butter to stand in for 1 cup of heavy cream. The mix lands close to the fat level of real cream, so a sauce stays rich instead of thinning out. It will not whip, so skip it for whipped toppings and ganache.

US cup, metric cup and UK cup

A US cup holds 236.6 millilitres, so 238 grams. A metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand and Canada, holds 250 millilitres, about 6 percent more, so about 251 grams and about 853 calories. A UK cup is also 250 millilitres in modern recipes, and British recipes usually call it double cream: 48 percent fat, about 242 grams a cup, and it whips the same way. When a recipe gives grams, use the grams and skip the cup entirely.

In a Pakistani kitchen

Cream turns up in Pakistani cooking in white handi, in creamy daal and karahi, and in desserts like fruit cream and trifle. The malai skimmed off boiled milk at home is richer and thicker than carton cream, and it is not the same as a measured cup of heavy cream, so weigh it if a recipe needs grams.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 cup of heavy cream?

238 grams of liquid heavy cream, about 809 calories, from USDA FoodData Central (FDC 170859).

How many grams is 1 cup of whipped cream?

120 grams, about 408 calories. One cup of liquid cream whips to about 2 cups, so the whipped cup holds about half the cream.

Why does King Arthur say 227 grams?

King Arthur groups heavy cream, light cream and half-and-half together at 227 grams, which is 8 ounces by weight. USDA measures heavy whipping cream on its own at 238 grams.

How many grams is a tablespoon of heavy cream?

About 15 grams, about 51 calories, from USDA FoodData Central (FDC 170859).

Why not 238 grams for every cup of cream?

Because cream changes with form. Liquid heavy cream is 238 grams a cup, whipped cream is 120 grams, and King Arthur groups heavy cream, light cream and half-and-half together at 227 grams.

How many grams is half a cup of liquid heavy cream?

119 grams, about 405 calories. A quarter cup is about 60 grams.

Every figure on this page is approximate, because brands and fat content vary a little, and how a cup is filled changes the weight. For exact work, weigh the cream liquid.
