How many grams in one roti?

A standard roti is made from a dough ball of about 35 to 45 grams, which comes from roughly 30 grams of atta. A small phulka uses a dough ball of about 30 grams, and a large roti about 55 grams. The cooked roti weighs a little less than the dough ball, because some water steams off on the tawa. The number to remember is 30 grams of atta per roti.

How much atta is in one roti?

The confusion starts because two different weights get quoted as one number. The dough ball includes water. The atta is only the flour. A standard roti's dough ball weighs about 35 to 45 grams, but only about 30 grams of that is atta, and the rest is water. Recipes that say 45 grams usually mean the dough ball. Pages that say 30 grams usually mean the flour. They are talking about different things.

Recipe sites that weigh their dough land in the same range: Cook With Manali divides its dough into balls of 35 to 37 grams, Culinary Shades uses 45-gram balls, and a chapati-making guide puts machine-divided balls at 30 to 40 grams. Why do different pages give different numbers?

Because one roti has no fixed size. One page's medium is another page's large. Some pages weigh the dough ball, others weigh the flour, and most never say which. A page that says 120 calories usually means a 40-gram piece. A page that says 80 calories usually means a 25-gram piece. Both can be right. A calorie number only means something with the weight next to it, so a figure with no weight is a guess.

How many calories in one roti?

For a plain roti with no ghee, the numbers are about 70 to 90 for a phulka, 100 to 120 for a standard roti, and 140 to 170 for a large one. The spread is wide because the size spread is wide. A teaspoon of ghee brushed on top adds about 45 calories, so a standard roti with ghee is about 145 to 165 calories, and two teaspoons add about 90.

Take the worked example from the TDEE guide: a 30-year-old man, 80 kilograms, 180 centimetres, moderately active, with a TDEE of 2,759 calories a day. Two standard plain rotis are about 200 to 240 calories, roughly 7 to 9 percent of that day. Three are about 300 to 360, roughly 11 to 13 percent. Ghee on each one adds about 45 calories, so three ghee rotis come to about 435 to 495. Knowing the size is what matters.

Why does the weight vary?

Atta brands absorb different amounts of water, so the same 30 grams of flour makes a wetter or drier dough. Wetter dough rolls thinner and cooks lighter. Thickness matters more than diameter: a thick small roti can outweigh a thin large one. Ghee in the dough or on top adds weight and calories. A tandoor roti is usually thicker and heavier than a tawa phulka. Weigh your own and the variation stops mattering.

Weigh it once and you know your roti

Make the dough as usual, then weigh the atta before adding water, or weigh the dough balls. If 250 grams of atta makes eight rotis, each one holds about 30 grams of atta and about 105 calories. That number holds for months, because your hands make the same size roti every time. After that you can log two rotis as about 210 calories without weighing anything again.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams of atta is one roti?

About 30 grams for a standard roti, 20 to 25 grams for a small phulka, and 35 to 50 grams for a large one. The dough ball weighs more, about 35 to 45 grams for a standard roti, because it includes water.

How many calories in one roti without ghee?

About 100 to 120 for a standard plain roti. A small phulka is about 70 to 90, and a large roti about 140 to 170. With one teaspoon of ghee, add about 45 calories.

Does a cooked roti weigh the same as the dough?

No, it weighs a little less, because water steams off on the tawa. The difference is small. For tracking, use the dough weight or the flour weight, not the cooked weight, because the flour is what carries the calories.
