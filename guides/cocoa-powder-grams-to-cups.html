How many grams in a cup of cocoa powder?

Last updated: 28 Nov 2026.

One level US cup of unsweetened cocoa powder weighs 86 grams, according to USDA FoodData Central (entry FDC 169593). That is the number to use when a recipe gives no brand. Other honest sources give slightly different numbers, 84 grams from King Arthur Baking and 82 grams from some conversion tables, and real brands spread wider still, from about 75 grams for a light natural cocoa to about 95 grams for a dense Dutch-process one. The gap comes from how the powder is packed into the cup, not from anyone being wrong.

Why the sources disagree: 82, 84, or 86 grams

Cocoa powder is a fine, light powder, so the way it goes into the cup changes the weight more than it does for sugar or flour. Spooning it in and levelling it off gives the lightest cup. Scooping the cup straight through the bag packs the powder down and can add 10 grams or more. Sifting first fluffs it up and takes a few grams off again.

The brand matters too. A cup of Hershey's natural cocoa weighs about 75 grams, while a cup of Valrhona Dutch-process cocoa weighs about 95 grams. That is a 20 gram spread inside one recipe, about 45 calories, decided before the oven is even on. When a recipe depends on the exact amount, weigh the cocoa on a scale and skip the cup entirely.

Natural cocoa vs Dutch-process cocoa

Both kinds weigh the same in the cup, about 86 grams, and both carry about the same calories, 196 for natural and about 189 for Dutch-process. The difference is acidity, not weight. Natural cocoa is acidic. Dutch-process cocoa has been washed with an alkali, which takes the acidity out and leaves a darker colour and a smoother, less sharp flavour.

The acidity decides the leavening. Natural cocoa needs baking soda, which is alkaline and reacts with the acid to lift the cake. Dutch-process cocoa has no acid left to react, so recipes written for it use baking powder instead. Swapping one cocoa for the other without changing the leavening can leave a cake flat or tasting soapy, even though the weight in the cup was exactly right. Colour gives it away: natural cocoa is a lighter reddish brown, Dutch-process is darker, sometimes almost black, and the packet usually says "processed with alkali" when it is Dutch-process.

How to measure cocoa powder

Fluff the powder with a spoon first, then spoon it into the measuring cup until it heaps above the rim, and level it off with the back of a knife. Do not tap the cup on the counter to settle it, and do not press it down. Tapping is the fastest way to add grams you did not plan for.

For baking, sift the cocoa with the flour instead of sifting it on its own. Cocoa clumps more than flour does, and the lumps survive mixing if they go in dry.

Worked example: chocolate cake for eight

A chocolate cake that calls for 3/4 cup of cocoa powder needs about 65 grams. If the recipe was written with a light natural cocoa at 75 grams a cup, the true amount is about 56 grams. If it was written with a dense Dutch-process cocoa at 95 grams a cup, the true amount is about 71 grams. Same recipe, same measuring cup, 15 grams apart.

Fifteen grams of cocoa is about 34 calories and a visible difference in how dark and how bitter the cake comes out. One weighing settles which side of the range your bag sits on, and the cake tastes the same every time you bake it.

Calories and nutrition in cocoa powder

One cup of unsweetened cocoa powder, 86 grams, carries about 196 calories. Per 100 grams it is 228 calories, with 17 grams of protein, 50 grams of carbohydrate, 32 grams of fibre, and 12 grams of fat in the cup. Most of the carbohydrate is fibre, which is why a spoonful of cocoa adds far more flavour than calories.

Those numbers are for the powder on its own. A sweetened cocoa mix, the kind sold for instant hot chocolate, is mostly sugar and weighs much more per cup, so none of these figures apply to it. Check the packet when the product has sugar, milk powder, or anything else added. If you are counting the day, work out what the portion means in the TDEE calculator.

US cup, metric cup, and UK cup

A US cup holds 236.6 millilitres and gives 86 grams. A metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand, and Canada, holds 250 millilitres, about 6 percent more, so the same cocoa weighs about 91 grams in it. A UK cup is also 250 millilitres in modern recipes, so use 91 grams there too. Spoons stay the same everywhere, a tablespoon 5.4 grams and a teaspoon 1.8 grams. When a recipe gives grams, use the grams and skip the cup entirely.

In a Pakistani kitchen

Cocoa powder turns up in Pakistani kitchens mostly in chocolate cake, brownies, and hot chocolate, and in chocolate-flavoured doodh for children. The packet on the shelf is usually a sweetened drinking-chocolate mix rather than pure unsweetened cocoa, so weigh it as the product it is and do not use the 86 gram figure for it. For pure cocoa in a cake, the same 86 grams a cup applies, and the Dutch-process kind is the one that gives the dark colour people expect from a bakery chocolate cake.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 cup of cocoa powder?

86 grams for unsweetened cocoa powder, spooned and levelled, from USDA FoodData Central. King Arthur Baking lists 84 grams, and brands range from about 75 to 95 grams a cup.

How many grams is 1 tablespoon of cocoa powder?

5.4 grams, with about 12 calories. A teaspoon is 1.8 grams.

Is Dutch-process cocoa heavier than natural cocoa?

No. Both weigh about 86 grams a cup. Dutch-process cocoa is darker and less acidic, and it needs baking powder rather than baking soda.

Should I sift cocoa powder before measuring?

Sift it with the flour when you bake, to break up lumps. Measure first and sift second, because sifting before measuring makes the cup lighter.

Why not 86 grams for every cup of cocoa powder?

Because brands spread from about 75 grams for a light natural cocoa to about 95 grams for a dense Dutch-process one. USDA lists 86 grams, King Arthur Baking lists 84 grams, and some conversion tables list 82 grams.

How many grams is half a cup of cocoa powder?

43 grams, spooned and levelled. A quarter cup is 22 grams.

Does cocoa powder weigh the same as drinking chocolate?

No. A sweetened cocoa mix is mostly sugar and weighs much more per cup, so none of these figures apply to it. Check the packet when the product has sugar, milk powder, or anything else added.

Every figure on this page is approximate, because brands, processing, and how a cup is filled change the weight. For exact work, weigh the cocoa powder.
