How many grams in a cup of sugar?

A US cup of granulated sugar is about 198 to 200 grams. King Arthur Baking lists 198 grams, and the USDA lists 200 grams. The same cup of flour is only 120 to 125 grams, so a cup of sugar weighs about 75 grams more than a cup of flour. The type of sugar moves the number more than the cup does: brown sugar pressed into the cup weighs more, powdered sugar much less.

The widest disagreements are for turbinado and caster sugar. King Arthur lists turbinado at 180 grams a cup and its superfine sugar at 190 grams, while other baking references list about 200 grams for both. For granulated, brown and powdered sugar, the two sources agree within a few grams.

Why do King Arthur and the USDA give different numbers?

They measure differently and round differently. King Arthur spoons the sugar into the cup and levels it, and lists 198 grams. The USDA lists 200 grams for the same cup. Two grams is 1 percent, smaller than the difference between two brands of sugar. Two grams will not change a recipe. The 70 grams that packing adds to brown sugar will, and so will the 20 grams that sifting takes off powdered sugar.

Why brown sugar and powdered sugar change so much

Brown sugar holds molasses, so the crystals stick together. Pressed firmly into the cup, a cup holds 213 to 220 grams. Spooned in loosely, the same cup holds only about 145 grams, a gap of about 70 grams. Recipes that say brown sugar almost always mean packed, so press it down until it holds the shape of the cup. Powdered sugar works the other way. It is ground fine and traps air, so a cup spooned in unsifted weighs 113 to 120 grams. Sifted, the same cup weighs about 100 grams. If a recipe says sifted, sift first and then measure. If it does not say, spoon it in unsifted.

What about a metric cup?

A metric cup holds 250 millilitres, not 236.6, so it holds about 6 percent more. A metric cup of granulated sugar is about 210 to 213 grams. The converter on this site gives 213 grams at its density of 0.85 grams per millilitre, and King Arthur's 198 grams scales to about 210 grams. For other sugars, add about 6 percent to the US cup figure. If a recipe from Australia or New Zealand says one cup of sugar, it usually means a metric cup, and 200 grams will leave you about 10 grams short.

Worked example

A cake recipe calls for 2 cups of granulated sugar. At 198 to 200 grams a cup, that is about 400 grams: 2 times 198 is 396, and 2 times 200 is 400. A recipe that lists 250 grams of sugar needs about 1 and a quarter cups: 250 divided by 200 is 1.25. A tablespoon of granulated sugar is about 12.5 grams, since 16 tablespoons fill a cup, and a teaspoon is about 4 grams. So 100 grams of sugar is 8 tablespoons, about half a cup.

How to measure a cup of sugar

Spoon granulated sugar into a dry measuring cup and level the top with the flat edge of a knife. Do not dip the cup into the bag: scooping packs the crystals and can add 20 to 30 grams. Press brown sugar firmly into the cup. Spoon powdered sugar in without packing it, and sift it only if the recipe says sifted. If a recipe gives weights in grams, skip the cup entirely and weigh the sugar. A kitchen scale removes the technique from the measurement.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 cup of sugar?

About 198 to 200 grams for granulated white sugar in a US cup. Brown sugar packed is 213 to 220 grams, and powdered sugar unsifted is 113 to 120 grams.

Is brown sugar heavier than white sugar?

Yes, when packed. A packed cup of brown sugar is 213 to 220 grams against 198 to 200 grams for granulated sugar. Spooned in loosely, brown sugar is lighter, about 145 grams a cup.

How many grams is a tablespoon of sugar?

About 12.5 grams of granulated sugar. A teaspoon is about 4 grams. For brown sugar packed, a tablespoon is about 13 to 14 grams, and for powdered sugar about 7 to 8 grams.

Every figure on this page is approximate, because sugar density varies by brand, grind, and humidity. For exact work, weigh the sugar. Try it yourself in the kitchen converter, then read how many grams are in a cup of flour.
