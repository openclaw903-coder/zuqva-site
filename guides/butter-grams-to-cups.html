How many grams in a cup of butter?

Last updated: 28 Nov 2026.

One level US cup of butter weighs 227 grams, from USDA FoodData Central. That is two sticks, or half a pound, and it carries about 1,628 calories. The number holds for regular butter, salted or unsalted, cut into the cup and levelled. It does not hold for whipped butter, and it does not hold for a metric cup, which is a different size. Those two cases are where most butter conversions go wrong.

Why one cup is 227 grams and when it is not

Salted and unsalted butter weigh the same. USDA lists salted butter, record 173410, and butter without salt, record 173430, with identical household weights: 14.2 grams a tablespoon, 113 grams a stick, 227 grams a cup. The salt changes the sodium, not the weight. Per 100 grams, salted butter carries 643 milligrams of sodium and unsalted butter carries 11 milligrams.

Whipped butter is a different case. USDA lists whipped butter, record 173411, at 9.4 grams a tablespoon, 76 grams for a portion the size of a stick, and 151 grams a cup. Whipping beats air into the butter, so a cup holds about a third less butter than a cup of regular butter. A cup of whipped butter carries about 1,083 calories against about 1,628 for regular butter. Recipes that call for a cup of butter mean regular butter. To get 227 grams of butter from a tub of whipped butter, use about 1.5 cups.

Butter is solid, so a cup measure depends on how the cup is filled. Cold butter cut to fit the cup packs with almost no air. Soft butter spooned in leaves gaps, and the same cup can weigh less. The wrapper marks on a stick are the reliable measure without a scale. Each stick is marked in tablespoons, so cut along the mark instead of guessing. Melting does not change the weight of the butter you started with, so weigh it before it goes in the pan.

Outside the US, butter usually comes in a 250 gram block, which is about 2.2 sticks and about 1.1 US cups. If a US recipe calls for 1 cup of butter, cut 227 grams from the block and keep the remaining 23 grams for toast. The block wrapper often has gram marks printed on it, so cut along the mark.

Worked example: a cake that calls for a cup of butter

A cake recipe calls for 1 cup of butter. That is 227 grams, two sticks, and about 1,628 calories of butter in the whole cake. Cut into 12 slices, the butter alone adds about 136 calories to each slice.

Use a 250 gram block without cutting it down and the whole block adds about 1,793 calories, about 165 calories more than the recipe asks for, so cut 23 grams off first. Use whipped butter by the cup instead and the cake gets about 545 calories less butter, and less fat than the recipe was written for, so the crumb turns out drier. Weighing the butter takes the form of the butter out of the recipe entirely.

Logging butter without logging the wrong amount

A cup of regular butter carries about 1,628 calories, 184 grams of fat, 117 grams of it saturated, and almost no protein or carbohydrate. A tablespoon on toast weighs 14.2 grams and adds about 102 calories, easy to forget when it happens every morning.

The entry has to match the form you weighed, the same as with dry and cooked grains. Log a cup of whipped butter as regular butter and you count about 1,628 calories instead of about 1,083, an overcount of more than 500 calories. US cup, metric cup and UK cup

A US cup holds 236.6 millilitres, so 227 grams of butter. A metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand and Canada, holds 250 millilitres, about 6 percent more, so about 240 grams of butter. A UK cup is also 250 millilitres in modern recipes, so use the metric figures there too. When a recipe gives grams, use the grams and skip the cup entirely.

In a Pakistani kitchen

Butter in Pakistani shops is usually sold by weight in blocks, not in sticks, so a recipe that calls for a cup means cutting 227 grams from the block. A 250 gram block is the common size, and it holds about 1.1 cups. Butter turns up most often in baking, on toast and in parathas. Desi ghee is clarified butter, with the water and milk solids removed, so it is a different ingredient by the cup and needs its own conversion instead of the butter figure.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 cup of butter?

227 grams for one level US cup of regular butter, from USDA FoodData Central. That is two sticks, or half a pound.

How many grams is 1 cup of whipped butter?

151 grams from USDA FoodData Central. Whipped butter holds air, so a cup weighs about a third less than a cup of regular butter.

How many grams is a stick of butter?

113 grams. One stick is half a cup, 8 tablespoons, or 4 ounces. Two sticks make a cup, 227 grams.

How many grams is a metric cup of butter?

About 240 grams. A metric cup holds 250 millilitres against 236.6 for a US cup, so it holds about 13 grams more butter.

How many grams is 100 grams of butter in cups?

About 0.44 of a cup, or 7 tablespoons. A full cup is 227 grams.

Does melted butter weigh the same as solid butter?

Yes. Melting does not change the weight of the butter you started with, so weigh it before it goes in the pan.

Every figure on this page is approximate, because brands and how a cup is filled vary a little, and soft butter spooned into a cup can weigh less than cold butter cut to fit. For exact work, weigh the butter.
