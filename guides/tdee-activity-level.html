How to choose your activity level for TDEE

Your TDEE is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor. The factor is 1.2 if you are sedentary, 1.375 if you are lightly active, 1.55 if you are moderately active, 1.725 if you are very active, and 1.9 if you are extremely active. The factor is the part of the calculation most people get wrong, and they usually pick one level too high. Pick the level that matches your normal week, not your best week.

What is the activity factor?

The activity factor turns BMR into TDEE. BMR is what you burn at rest. The factor adds everything else: walking, working, training, and the small movements you make all day without thinking about them. A sedentary factor of 1.2 adds 20 percent on top of BMR. An extremely active factor of 1.9 nearly doubles it. That is why two people with the same BMR can have TDEEs more than 1,000 calories apart.

The five activity levels

Read the descriptions and pick the one that sounds like your ordinary week. If two levels both sound close, take the lower one. It is easier to eat a little more later than to spend weeks wondering why the scale does not move.

LevelFactorWhat it looks like
Sedentary1.2Desk job, little or no exercise, most of the day sitting.
Lightly active1.375Desk job with light exercise one to three days a week, or a job that keeps you on your feet part of the day.
Moderately active1.55Moderate exercise three to five days a week, or a job with regular walking and standing.
Very active1.725Hard exercise six or seven days a week, or a physical job plus regular training.
Extremely active1.9Very hard exercise every day, often twice a day, or heavy manual work plus training.

A worked example

Take the same example the calculator uses. A 30-year-old man, 80 kilograms, 180 centimetres. His BMR is 1,780 calories a day. Multiply that by each factor and the TDEE changes a lot: sedentary 2,136, lightly active 2,448, moderately active 2,759, very active 3,071, extremely active 3,382. The gap between the lowest and the highest is 1,246 calories, which is why choosing honestly matters: one level too high adds about 300 calories a day to your target, which is enough to stall fat loss for weeks.

Three mistakes that make your TDEE too high

The first mistake is adding exercise calories on top of the activity factor. The factor already includes your training, so adding the calories your watch reports for the same workout counts that workout twice. If you train four days a week, the moderately active factor already covers it. Add exercise calories only if a calculator asks for BMR alone, which this one does not.

The second mistake is using the same number for months. After losing weight your body is smaller and burns less, so the same factor gives a lower TDEE. A 10-kilogram loss lowers BMR by about 100 calories a day, and TDEE by a little more, so the old target slowly stops working. Recalculate every month or two, or after every few kilograms lost.

The third mistake is forgetting that activity falls when you eat less. During a cut you walk less, fidget less, and take the lift instead of the stairs, mostly without noticing. Your real activity level drops a notch while the calculator still uses the old one. If fat loss stalls for two or three weeks, check how much you are moving before you cut more food.

Frequently asked questions

I go to the gym three times a week. What level am I?

Moderately active, if you sit at a desk the rest of the day. Three sessions a week with a desk job is the usual case for 1.55. If your job also keeps you moving, you may be very active. Judge the whole week, not the gym days alone.

Should I pick a higher level to be safe?

No. A higher level raises your calorie target, so eating to it slows fat loss or turns a cut into maintenance. If you are between two levels, take the lower one. You can always eat more later, and it is easier to add food than to take it away.

How often should I recalculate my TDEE?

Whenever something changes: your weight, your training, or your job. Weight matters most, because a smaller body burns less. Check your numbers every month or two, and always after a big change in routine.
