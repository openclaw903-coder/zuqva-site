How many grams in a cup of pasta?

Last updated: 28 Nov 2026.

One level US cup of dry pasta weighs 64 to 122 grams, depending on the shape: shells are the lightest at 64 grams and elbow macaroni the heaviest at 122, both from USDA FoodData Central. Cooked, a loosely filled cup lands between 105 and 124 grams for every shape. Shape matters most before cooking and barely matters after it.

Why dry pasta cups weigh so differently

Dry pasta is a pile of rigid shapes, and a cup measures the air between them as much as the pasta. A shell traps air inside itself, so a cup of shells is mostly empty space at 64 grams. An elbow nests into the next one, so the same cup holds nearly twice as much at 122 grams. Spaghetti lies flat at 91 grams, penne tubes leave hollow centres at 95, rotini spirals sit between at 96.

A recipe that says a cup of pasta without naming the shape is guessing by up to 58 grams, about 215 calories, before anything is cooked.

Cooked pasta: loose cups and packed cups

Cooking changes the maths: the shapes soften, fill with water, and pack alike, so a loosely filled cup weighs 105 to 124 grams for every shape, an 18 percent spread against 90 percent dry. Spaghetti is heaviest at 124 grams, shells lightest at 105.

Packing matters as much as shape. USDA lists cooked spaghetti at 124 grams a cup unpacked and 151 grams packed, 22 percent more of it and about 43 more calories, decided by how hard you press with the spoon. Cooked elbows go from 120 grams loose to 132 packed.

How much dry pasta per person

The standard serving is 2 ounces of dry pasta per person, about 56 grams, which Barilla and the USDA both use, cooking into about a cup to a cup and a quarter. Spaghetti is the tidiest: 2 ounces cooks into almost exactly 1 cup. Penne needs about two thirds of a cup dry and gives about 1 and a quarter cups cooked; elbow macaroni needs about half a cup dry and gives about 1 and an eighth.

A 2-ounce serving carries about 208 calories. Cooking adds water, and the calories stay the same, spread over more grams. If you are tracking, weigh it dry.

Worked example: spaghetti for four

Spaghetti for four: four servings at 2 ounces each is 8 ounces, 224 grams, about two and a half cups dry. Cooked, that is about four cups, around 525 grams, at about 831 calories for the pot, or 208 each before sauce. Two and a half cups of dry elbows by mistake would weigh about 305 grams and carry about 1,132 calories, about 300 more.

Dry versus cooked calories, and the logging trap

Dry pasta carries 371 calories per 100 grams, cooked pasta 158, because more than half its weight is water from the pot. The calories do not change when pasta cooks; they spread over more grams. One hundred grams dry comes out weighing about 235 grams, with the same 371 calories it went in with.

Weigh cooked pasta and log it with a dry entry and you count more than double the real calories. Weigh dry pasta and log it with a cooked entry and you count less than half. Pick the entry that matches the state you weighed it in; if you are not sure, weigh it dry, before it goes in the pot. Work out what the portion means for your day in the TDEE calculator.

Whole-wheat pasta has the same cup weights but 352 calories per 100 grams dry, and a cup of its cooked spaghetti weighs 117 grams and carries about 174 calories.

US cup, metric cup, and UK cup

A US cup holds 236.6 millilitres; a metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand, and Canada, holds 250, about 6 percent more, so add about 6 percent to every figure here: dry spaghetti goes from 91 grams to about 96, dry elbows from 122 to about 129. A UK cup is also 250 millilitres in modern recipes. When a recipe gives grams, use the grams and skip the cup entirely.

In a Pakistani kitchen

In Pakistani cooking, macaroni almost always means elbow macaroni, a cup of it dry about 122 grams. It shows up in masala macaroni, pasta chaat, and lunchbox pasta, usually cooked soft and mixed with sauce, so use the loose cooked cup, about 120 grams. If a recipe says a cup of macaroni without saying dry or cooked, it usually means dry, measured before the pot.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 cup of dry pasta?

Between 64 and 122 grams, depending on the shape: shells 64 grams, elbows 122, the rest in between, all from USDA FoodData Central.

How many grams is 1 cup of cooked pasta?

Between 105 and 124 grams for a loosely filled cup: shells 105 grams, penne and rotini 107, elbows 120, spaghetti 124. Press it down and spaghetti reaches 151 grams.

How much dry pasta should I cook per person?

Two ounces, about 56 grams, of dry pasta per person. That cooks into about a cup for spaghetti and about a cup and a quarter for penne.

Why not one number for a cup of dry pasta?

Because shape decides how much fits in the cup. Shells are the lightest at 64 grams a cup and elbow macaroni the heaviest at 122 grams, both from USDA FoodData Central.

How many grams is 1 cup of dry spaghetti?

91 grams. Cooked, a loosely filled cup of spaghetti is 124 grams.

How many grams is 1 cup of cooked penne?

107 grams for a loosely filled cup. Dry penne is 95 grams a cup.

Every figure on this page is approximate, because brands, shapes, and cooking times vary a little, and how a cup is filled changes the weight. For exact work, weigh the pasta dry.
