How many grams in a cup of lentils?

Last updated: 28 Nov 2026.

One level US cup of dry lentils weighs 192 grams and carries about 676 calories, from USDA FoodData Central. Cooked, one level cup weighs 198 grams and carries about 230 calories. The two cups look almost the same on the scale, yet they hold very different amounts of food.

Dry and cooked lentils by type

Every figure is for one level US cup, 236.6 millilitres. Dry figures are USDA's raw lentils entry (FDC 172420), 352 calories per 100 grams, and cooked figures are USDA's boiled lentils entry (FDC 172421), 116 calories per 100 grams. Red lentils run a little higher dry because their raw entry is 358 calories per 100 grams against 352 for brown and green. Once cooked, the type matters less: a cup of cooked lentils is about 198 grams and 230 calories whichever type went into the pot. French green lentils keep their shape and give fewer cups because their smaller seeds swell less.

Why one dry cup makes 2.5 to 3 cooked cups

Ask five cooks how many cups one cup of dry lentils makes and you get five answers: 2, 2.5, 3. They are all measuring the same pot. A cup of dry lentils drinks in water as it cooks, roughly two to two and a half times its own weight, and how much water stays in decides the cup count. Cook brown lentils firm for a salad and you get about 2.5 cups; cook them soft for a soup and you get closer to 3.

The cup count and the cup weight move together. If your pot gives 2.5 cups, each cup weighs more than 198 grams; if it gives 3, each cup lands near 198 grams. Either way the pot holds the same 676 calories it started with. Water adds weight and volume and no calories.

Use about 3 cups of liquid for each cup of dry lentils on the stove, and start checking brown and green lentils at 20 minutes. Red lentils need about 2.5 cups when you are not draining them, and they break down into a soft mash in 15 to 20 minutes. Lentils never need soaking, and older lentils take longer than fresh ones, so taste one to check.

How much dry lentils per person

As a main dish, half a cup of dry lentils per person is a good serving: 96 grams, about 338 calories, cooking into about 1.25 to 1.5 cups. As a side, a quarter cup dry is enough: 48 grams, about 169 calories, cooking into about two thirds of a cup. A cup of cooked lentils carries about 18 grams of protein and 16 grams of fibre, plus about 6.6 milligrams of iron, all from USDA's boiled lentils entry.

One cup of dry lentils gives 5 to 6 half cup servings cooked. Cooked lentils keep about 5 days in the fridge. A standard 400 gram can holds about 1.5 cups drained, so one cup dry is a little more than one and a half cans.

Worked example: lentils for four

Lentils for four as a main: 2 cups dry, 384 grams, about 1,352 calories. Add about 6 cups of water, simmer brown lentils 20 to 30 minutes, and the pot gives 5 to 6 cups cooked, about 1.25 to 1.5 cups and 338 calories each before anything else goes in. Use red lentils instead and the same 2 cups dry gives about 5 cups cooked in 15 to 20 minutes, softer and thicker, with about 1,374 calories in the pot.

If a recipe calls for 1 cup of cooked lentils, start with about a third of a cup dry; for 4 cups cooked, start with about 1.5 cups dry. Weighing is the reliable way: a cup of dry lentils is 192 grams, and a scale removes the guesswork.

Dry versus cooked calories, and the logging trap

Dry lentils carry 352 calories per 100 grams and cooked lentils 116, because more than half the cooked weight is water from the pot. The calories do not change when lentils cook; they spread over more grams. One hundred grams dry comes out weighing about 300 grams cooked, with the same 352 calories it went in with.

Weigh cooked lentils and log them with a dry entry and you count about three times the real calories; weigh dry and log them cooked and you count about a third. Pick the entry that matches the state you weighed them in. Work out what the portion means for your day in the TDEE calculator.

US cup, metric cup, and UK cup

A US cup holds 236.6 millilitres; a metric cup, used in Australia, New Zealand, and Canada, holds 250, about 6 percent more, so add about 6 percent to every figure here: dry lentils go from 192 grams to about 204, cooked from 198 to about 210. A UK cup is also 250 millilitres in modern recipes. When a recipe gives grams, use the grams and skip the cup entirely.

In a Pakistani kitchen

In Pakistani cooking, lentils usually mean masoor dal, the red split lentil: a cup dry is about 192 grams, and it cooks soft in 15 to 20 minutes with about 2.5 cups of water, breaking down into the smooth dal that takes a tarka. Brown lentils show up as sabut masoor, holding their shape longer and needing the full 20 to 30 minutes. If a recipe says a cup of dal without saying dry or cooked, it usually means dry, measured before the pot. Moong and chana dal are different lentils with their own weights, so do not use these figures for them.

Frequently asked questions

How many grams is 1 cup of dry lentils?

192 grams, about 676 calories for brown or green lentils and about 687 for red, from USDA FoodData Central.

How many grams is 1 cup of cooked lentils?

198 grams, about 230 calories, with about 18 grams of protein and 16 grams of fibre, from USDA's boiled lentils entry.

How many cups cooked does 1 cup of dry lentils make?

About 2.5 to 3 cups, depending on the type and how soft you cook them: brown and green give 2.5 to 3, red split about 2.5, French green 2 to 2.5.

How many grams is 1 cup of red lentils?

192 grams dry, the same as brown or green lentils. Cooked, a cup is 198 grams for every type.

How long do lentils take to cook?

Red split lentils take 15 to 20 minutes, brown or green take 20 to 30 minutes, and French green lentils take 30 to 40 minutes.

Why do dry and cooked lentils weigh almost the same per cup?

Because the cooked cup is mostly water. The dry cup holds about 676 calories and the cooked cup about 230 calories, so the two cups look almost the same on the scale yet hold very different amounts of food.

Every figure on this page is approximate, because brands, varieties, and cooking times vary a little, and how a cup is filled changes the weight. For exact work, weigh the lentils dry.
