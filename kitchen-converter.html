Every result here is APPROXIMATE. Density varies by brand, grind, moisture and how tightly an ingredient is packed or scooped. For baking, a kitchen scale beats any volume conversion. Use these numbers to follow a recipe, not to measure medicine or anything where an exact dose matters.

Why grams ↔ millilitres is not a fixed ratio

The missing number is density: grams per millilitre. Volume = grams ÷ density, and grams = millilitres × density. Water is the one easy case, at 1.00 g/ml, so 100 g of water is exactly 100 ml. Flour is mostly air between particles, so 100 g of all-purpose flour is about 193 ml. Honey is dense, so 100 g of honey is only about 70 ml. Same weight, very different volume — which is why a recipe that says “1 cup” can fail if you swap ingredients by cup alone.

Sources and notes: all-purpose flour uses the guide figure, 122.5 g per US cup, the midpoint of King Arthur 120 and USDA 125, giving 0.518 g/ml. Granulated sugar uses the guide figure, 199 g per cup, the midpoint of USDA 200 and King Arthur 198, giving 0.841 g/ml. Butter is about 0.96, honey about 1.42 and oil about 0.91. Whole-wheat flour is widely listed at 120 g per US cup (236.6 ml), giving 0.507 g/ml. Coarse semolina is listed at 167 g per US cup, giving 0.706 g/ml. A US cup is 236.6 ml throughout this site. New rows use the same method: the guide cup weight divided by 236.6 ml. Heavy cream is 238 g a cup from USDA FoodData Central (FDC 170859), giving 1.006. Cocoa powder is 86 g a cup from USDA (FDC 169593), giving 0.363. Dry lentils are 192 g a cup from USDA (FDC 172420), giving 0.811. Dry quinoa is 170 g a cup from USDA (FDC 168874), giving 0.719. Loosely packed shredded cheddar is 113 g a cup from USDA, giving 0.478. Standard semisweet chocolate chips are 170 g a cup from King Arthur Baking, giving 0.719. Dry long-grain white rice is 185 g a cup from USDA, giving 0.782. Dry rolled oats are 81 g a cup from USDA, giving 0.342. Beaten egg is 243 g a cup, giving 1.027. Corn syrup is 1.390 g/ml, the specific gravity for light or dark corn syrup in the Food Standards Australia New Zealand specific gravity table, which is also the figure in the FAO density database. Powdered sugar uses the midpoint of the guide range for unsifted powdered sugar, 113 to 120 g a cup, which is 116.5 g and gives 0.492. Brown sugar uses the midpoint of the guide range for packed brown sugar, 213 to 220 g a cup, which is 216.5 g and gives 0.915. King Arthur Baking lists powdered sugar at 113 g a cup and packed brown sugar at 213 g a cup, the lower end of each range. Vegetable oil is 0.920, the representative figure for the published 0.91 to 0.93 range for common vegetable oils at room temperature, from the Codex Alimentarius relative density ranges. Dry pasta uses the midpoint of the guide range across shapes, 64 to 122 g a cup, which is 93 g and gives 0.393, so check the guide table for your shape.

Two ingredients deliberately omitted. Ghee is omitted because no single reliable density was found — it is solid when cool and liquid when warm, so its volume changes with temperature. Besan (chickpea flour) is omitted because published cup weights conflict sharply (about 85 g per cup in one systematic chart versus about 140 g per cup in a dip-and-sweep chart), so publishing one number would be misleading. If a future source resolves either figure, the ingredient will be added with that source named.

Worked example

100 g all-purpose flour ÷ 0.518 g/ml = 193.1 ml, rounded to 193 ml, which is 193 ÷ 236.6 = 0.82 US cups. Try 100 g of all-purpose flour in the converter above — you should get the same answer.
